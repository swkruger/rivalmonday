## What this page tells you

Whether Google search finds {{client}} and its top rivals on the major online directories for the client's business type, such as Facebook, Better Business Bureau and Yellow Pages. Client users see the same page.

- **Your listings** has one row per directory. **Listed** means Google search showed {{client}}'s listing on that directory, **Not found in Google search** means it did not, and **Not checked yet** means that directory has not been checked.
- For a listed row, the page shows whether the **name** and **phone** on the listing match {{client}}'s Google profile, and a link to the listing.
- **You vs top rivals** puts {{client}} next to up to 3 top rivals, directory by directory. Without a Top rival yet, the 3 tracked rivals that overlap most are used.
- **Also found on** lists other sites where Google search showed {{client}}'s listing with the same name and phone. It only appears when there is something to show.

## How to use it

1. **Start with the gaps.** Each **Not found in Google search** row is a directory where customers searching Google are unlikely to find {{client}}. Three or more gaps also put a "Get listed on the directories you're missing" recommendation on the board.
2. **Add the missing listings.** **Add listing** opens that directory's own sign-up page. Use exactly the same business name and phone as the Google profile.
3. **Fix what differs.** Where a row says **Name differs** or **Shows a different phone**, select **Fix on** that directory. It opens the directory's owner sign-in or claim page, and a short line under it says what to update so the listing matches the Google profile. Rivals' rows never get fix links.
4. **Use the comparison with the client.** A directory where the rivals are listed and {{client}} is not makes a clear, concrete talking point.

## Common questions

### How often is this checked?

Once a month, for {{client}} and its top rivals. The date under **Your listings** shows when the last check finished. The first check runs once the client's competitors are set. You can also run one yourself with **Check listings now**.

### What does Check listings now do?

It searches again for {{client}}'s own listings on every directory, and reuses recent results for the rivals. The page updates in a few minutes, and a new check also moves the next monthly check back. A check runs a few Google searches (a few cents), counts toward the client's monthly cost guard, and is not billed to the client. Only agency users see the button; the client's users never do.

### How often can I use Check listings now?

Once per client every 7 days, and 10 checks per agency each day (UTC). The button shows the next available date, or says why it can't run: a check is already running, the client's own Google listing or its competitors aren't set yet, or the client's AI work is paused for the month.

### What does "Not found in Google search" mean?

We search Google for the business by name and phone, then by name and city, and look for results from each directory. "Not found" means none of those results came from that directory. The business may still be listed there, but the listing does not show up when customers search Google.

### Why does it show a different phone?

The directory shows a different number than the Google profile. It may be a call-tracking number, so check with the client before changing anything. Toll-free numbers are never reported as a different phone.

### Is the address checked?

Not yet. Each listed row says **Address: not checked**. Only the name and phone are compared for now.

### Why are the client's own listings missing?

The page needs {{client}}'s own Google business on file. Add the Google Place ID under **Client settings › Business profile**; the client's listings appear after the next monthly check. Until then the page shows a note, and the comparison shows the rivals only.

### Which directories are checked?

The major directories for the client's business type: general ones for every business, plus trade directories (for example Angi and HomeAdvisor for home services, or the ADA's Find-a-Dentist for dental practices).
